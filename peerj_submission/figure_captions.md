# Figure captions

**Figure 1. The locked paired-validation protocol.** Three stages, each with three components. The design stage fixes the atlas (three seeds × four model families × 32 draws × 12 scenarios = 4,608 pairs per controller), the scoring rule (a logistic boundary fitted on independent seeds and then applied unchanged), and the four controller families. The inference stage pairs every controller trial with the matching baseline trial, applies cluster-robust bootstrap and permutation inference with Holm correction and minimum detectable effects, and reports multiple endpoints rather than a single success rate. The reporting stage adds a specification curve, falsification checks that refuse a winner from one rate or one weighting, and a reproducible artefact in which one entry point regenerates every table and figure.

**Figure 2. Paired change in success probability by controller and predicted-baseline-success band.** Points are paired risk differences against delayed full feedback; horizontal lines are 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals from 10,000 resamples of 384 clusters. The dashed line marks no change. Bands are terciles of a frozen logistic scoring rule for predicted baseline success, fitted on independent seeds. All four controllers are non-positive in the all-trial panel. In the predicted-low band the three plausible controllers have positive point estimates, but every interval includes zero and none survives Holm correction (adjusted p = 0.629 for all three). In the predicted-high band three controllers significantly reduce success.

**Figure 3. Failure-mode and energy endpoints.** Paired changes against delayed full feedback at the all-trial level, with 95% cluster-bootstrap intervals. Panels show thermal failure, ROS failure, overheating and delivered energy. Hypoxic failure did not change for any controller (all deltas exactly 0.000) and is omitted. The panels move in different directions: smoothed predictive is the only controller that reduces overheating, but it increases thermal failure; thermal-floor predictive and reduced-order MPC reduce thermal and ROS failure at the cost of more overheating and more delivered energy.

**Figure 4. Pareto structure and rank stability.** Left and centre panels plot each controller's change in success probability against its change in overheating rate and against its change in delivered energy. No controller dominates another on the five objectives, so all four are Pareto optimal. Right panel shows the probability that each controller ranks first under 200,000 weight vectors drawn from a uniform Dirichlet distribution over the five min-max-normalised objectives. Thermal-floor predictive ranks first most often, at 38.5%, but no controller is preferred under a majority of defensible weightings.

**Figure 5. Specification curve for the headline success contrast.** Each panel shows the paired change in success probability for one controller across 16 specifications, formed by crossing four seed subsets (all three seeds, or each seed alone) with four scenario subsets (all scenarios, nominal only, delayed only, noisy only), all evaluated with the paired cluster-bootstrap estimator. Points are sorted within each panel. Green points are positive and red points are negative. The fraction of specifications with a positive estimate is 37.5% for constrained optimized, 25% for smoothed predictive, 25% for thermal-floor predictive and 0% for reduced-order MPC.
